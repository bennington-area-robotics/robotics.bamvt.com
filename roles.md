# Roles and Responsibilities

Bennington Area Robotics organizes its work around three functions: **mechanical, controls,
and operations**. Each function includes three capabilities the team develops and
uses throughout the season.

Students can contribute across functions and build new skills over time. A student
might primarily write software, help run an outreach workshop, and scout at
competitions. Coaches and mentors help students find useful work, learn the skills
it requires, and take on responsibility as their experience grows.

## Mechanical {#mechanical}

Mechanical develops and maintains the physical robot.

- **Design and CAD:** Develop mechanism concepts, build prototypes, and use
  computer-aided design to plan parts, assemblies, and how everything fits together.
- **Fabrication and assembly:** Make parts, assemble mechanisms, and integrate them
  into the robot, working with controls on mounting, wiring access, and sensors.
- **Testing and maintenance:** Test mechanical performance and reliability, inspect
  and repair the robot, and identify needed tools and spare parts.

## Controls {#controls}

Controls connects the robot's electrical systems and software so it can operate
reliably under driver control and autonomously.

- **Electrical and sensors:** Plan and install wiring and power distribution, select
  and integrate sensors, and maintain reliable electrical connections.
- **Software:** Develop driver controls, autonomous routines, computer vision, and
  mechanism automation.
- **Testing and tuning:** Debug robot behavior, calibrate sensors, tune motion, and
  measure performance using test results and driver feedback.

## Operations {#operations}

Operations coordinates the team's work, resources, and relationships.

- **Planning and coordination:** Track priorities, schedules, task ownership, and
  dependencies; coordinate meeting plans, robot access, and event logistics.
- **Outreach and communications:** Organize community activities and relationships
  with other teams; produce photos, video, graphics, website updates, and the team's
  portfolio using contributions from all functions.
- **Finance and resources:** Develop budgets, organize fundraising, seek and maintain
  sponsorships, and track purchasing and inventory with coaches and program adults.

## Shared responsibilities {#shared-responsibilities}

**Strategy connects all three functions.** The team studies the game, chooses
priorities, and uses scouting and practice results to guide robot design, software,
and competition preparation.

**Documentation comes from everyone.** Students record the decisions, tests,
failures, and improvements in their own work. Operations coordinates the portfolio's
editing and presentation, with contributors checking that it accurately explains
what they did and learned.

**Competition assignments draw from across the team.** Driving, scouting, pit work,
and presenting to judges have specific responsibilities and preparation needs.
Students can take on these assignments alongside their regular work.

## Student leadership {#student-leadership}

A **project manager** coordinates work across the three functions: helping the team
set priorities, tracking progress and dependencies, and making sure students have
clear next tasks and opportunities to contribute. This includes arranging time for
building, programming, testing, and driver practice on the shared robot.

When the team uses a **co-lead project manager**, the two students agree on a clear
division of responsibilities so teammates know whom to approach. A **function lead**,
where useful, coordinates work and supports learning within mechanical, controls,
or operations. Students in leadership roles can also contribute directly to projects.

Coaches and mentors support student planning, provide instruction and feedback, and
help resolve problems. Adults retain responsibility for supervision, safety, and
program financial approvals. Participation and shop practices follow our
[Code of Conduct](/code-of-conduct/).
